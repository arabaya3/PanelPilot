# M5: routed connection lines (first slice)

## 1. What is built

The connection drawing (`connection_drawing_pdf`) keeps every M4 cross-reference sheet, which stays the authoritative record of every row, end, wire value and open item. After them come **routed sheets**: per board, its components and strips as boxes, every terminal a dot, and every saved `Connection` drawn as exactly one line between its two terminal dots. Then the open-items, full-text, sheet-index and findings sheets, as before.

Generated from the saved snapshot only, through the same single read and the same `DrawingSet` as the schedules (`drawing_set(model).routed`). No device, family, configuration or customer is named in layout or renderer code (`test_the_held_out_configuration_is_data_only` now covers `connection_routing.py`).

Not built in this slice (the proposal's M5a schema hints): pin sides, symbol parts, strip anchors, placement or routing hints, net display hints. No schema change: every snapshot, digest and subject digest is unchanged. The layout below uses only what the model already holds; the hints would only refine it later.

## 3. Layout rules (all fixed rules over data)

- **Placement.** Per board (a board never shares a routed sheet), in schedule order. Blocks are grouped by connected component of the board's own connections; a group that fits the current sheet stays together, else it starts a new sheet, else (larger than a sheet) it is placed block by block in breadth-first order of its connections and split by capacity (a block too tall continues in the other column or on the next sheet, "continued from / continues on sheet n, zone XY"). Components prefer the left column, strips the right; a full column continues in the other.
- **Boxes.** Title `<board>/<designation> (component|strip)`, in full; a long one wraps, with `(component)` or `(strip)` always on its last line (board-qualified so equal designations on different boards are not confused), the template, the symbol's review status (`symbol unreviewed: its drawing asserts nothing`, `symbol reviewed (template approved)`), or a dashed `symbol not defined` placeholder, and where the block's rows are on the cross-reference sheets. Each terminal row: its marking beside its dot, and a red `UNREALISED` / `UNCONNECTED` tag where the row has that note. Symbols themselves are drawn on the cross-reference sheets only, with their leads.
- **Dots.** A terminal point is a filled dot of 1.4 mm; a terminal with two or more connections (counted in the model) gets a filled junction dot of 2.2 mm, centred on the same point, replacing it. Dots sit 1.6 mm outside their box's edge, facing the channel. A dot that would fall within 1.5 mm of a zone row's edge is moved to 1.5 mm from it (its marking, centred on the dot, moves with it), so the dot and its marking sit in one zone; its lines then leave it by a short vertical piece. Where that move brings it within 3.6 mm of a neighbouring dot of its block, the rows below move down, each only as far as needed (the move fades out within two or three rows); where the box's foot stops that, the rows move up from the foot instead; a row pushed within 1.5 mm of a zone line goes on past it; a block with no room keeps its places. On the committed and stress cases adjacent dots of a block are now at least 3.6 mm apart (were 2.7 mm).
- **Fan-out.** A terminal with k connections takes k rows of 4.2 mm; its lines leave the dot at points inside the dot (offsets of 0.35 mm), one per row: up to three up, three down and one straight (seven). Since the final review every line leaves its dot horizontally on the dot's own height (each starts inside the dot within 0.4 mm of its centre, higher rows higher), then turns to its row 1.3 mm outside the dot and more (0.5 mm apart; the line going farthest turns nearest, so the fan never crosses itself); the rows are placed around the dot as finally placed (after any zone-line move): one line straight; two 0.9 mm above and below it, the upper label above and the lower label below, the junction taking three rows; three to seven a row apart, centred on it. Rows next to a two-line junction keep 7.7 mm from its dot (3.85 mm otherwise). A label (or unrouted-stub text) that would come within 1.6 mm vertically of one already placed whose extent it overlaps moves on along its own line, past the other's end, so adjacent rows' labels read side by side.
- **Lines.** Dot, fan-out, a horizontal escape (labelled), the connection's own vertical lane in the central channel, an escape, the other dot. Lanes: left-left pairs packed from the channel's left, right-right from its right, left-right centred, one lane each, ordered by insertion so each adds the fewest crossings. Same-side lanes are ordered by insertion too: the continuation lanes of that side keep their order (so tracks never cross lanes in the band), and each packed left-left (right-right) lane, shortest first, goes where the crossings of that side's escapes with its lanes are fewest (ties: farther from the column, so the shorter stays nearer). Two left-left or right-right runs share a lane only with 1.5 mm between them and never when they meet at one terminal (one lane through a junction would read as one line). Lanes are 3.5 mm apart. A sheet takes no more blocks than leave one lane for every connection touching it: a denser group continues on another sheet with flags instead of tighter lanes. Left and right rows sit on grids offset by half a row, so no two escapes are collinear.
- **Crossings.** Only a horizontal piece and a vertical piece of different connections cross, at a right angle; the horizontal one hops over the vertical one (a Bézier bump, 1.4 mm wide, leaving at least 2.1 mm between hops on adjacent lanes). No dot is ever drawn on a crossing; lines meet only inside a terminal's dot.
- **Continuations.** A far end on another sheet (another board, or a board split over sheets): the line runs down its lane to its own track in a band at the sheet's foot and on to a filled arrow flag at the frame edge (left-column lines to the left edge, right-column lines to the right), with `to <board>/<designation>:<marking> (sheet n, zone XY)` (and `, inner side` / `, outer side` after it for a strip point) above its end. The other sheet has the matching flag. A flag text is never cut: a long one wraps over several lines (sharing a left edge) above its track, and the track takes the room it needs, plus 1 mm so the text sits nearer its own track than the one above. Tracks are ordered so that tracks never cross lanes in the band.
- **Unrouted.** Beyond seven connections at one terminal, or when the channel has no free lane (the run with the longest span gives way first), the connection is not routed on any sheet: an arrowed 0.3 pt stub with `<label> not routed` after its arrowhead, on the stub's own line and 1.2 mm clear of the tip, where an end has a fan row (at an overcrowded terminal, one row per connection beyond the fan naming it, `w-… not routed`, and a row `(see the open items)`), its M4 stubs and cross-references unchanged, and an `unrouted` open item naming its label, both ends and the reason. The cover counts lines, continuations and unrouted (by reason).
- **Labels.** Above each escape: `w-` and a prefix of the wire id, or `x-` and a prefix of the connection id when there is no wire record; the shortest prefix of at least six characters no other connection or wire id of the model starts with.

## 4. PDF contract (what the independent read-back tests rely on)

- Terminal points: filled round dots under 3 mm (1.4 mm). Junction dots: filled round dots of 2.2 mm (1.57 times), centred on the terminal point, only at terminals with two or more connections, on every drawing sheet (cross-reference sheets too). Junction dots are drawn wherever the terminal is drawn, a continued M4 row (`(cont.)`) included.
- Routed lines: each connection is one stroked path (straight pieces and Bézier hops); its two ends lie inside its two endpoint dots, or one inside a dot and one at a continuation flag. Stroke width 0.35 pt, used for nothing else (symbols 0.5 pt, leads and stubs 0.3 pt, frames 0.6/0.3 pt). Jumper, internal and busbar lines (every via but wire and cable core, from the model's vocabulary) are dashed 5/2 pt; wire and cable-core lines solid.
- Crossings: orthogonal, with a hop; no dot within reach. Two unrelated collinear line ends are never within 3 mm.
- Continuation flag: a filled triangle 2 mm long at the frame edge, with the text `to <board>/<label> (sheet n, zone XY)` within 3 mm of the line's end.
- Line label: `w-…` or `x-…` (6 or more hex digits, a unique id prefix) 0.5 mm above its escape, within 1 mm of its line; it overlaps no other line or text.
- Block headers contain `(component)` or `(strip)`; the right column's header starts within 3 mm of its dots.
- Dotted stubs (dash `0.6 2.4`) and dash-dot stubs (`6 2 0.6 2`), each 8 mm, on the cross-reference sheets. The `symbol not defined` placeholder outline is dashed `3 2` (was `2 2`), so every two dash patterns of different meaning differ by at least 0.4 mm when an A3 sheet is printed fit-to-page on A4 (×0.707).
- Every routed sheet's top line starts `ROUTED CONNECTION SHEET: line paths are schematic` and ends with the print note: `Intended for A3 printing at 100%. Printing fit-to-page on A4 reduces text below the readability target.` The cover carries the same note under the disclaimer. It states the product's readability target, not a standard.

## 5. Readability (a product target at A3, not a standard)

Routed sheets: 7 pt for markings, labels, continuation texts and notes, 6 pt for the grey template/review lines, 8 pt titles. Cross-reference sheets: 6 pt body, and (since the print-size review) 6 pt for the grey wire and net detail lines too; pin markings and a symbol's own text at a symbol stay at 5 pt. This is the product readability target. At A3, 7 pt Helvetica has a cap height of about 1.8 mm; labels sit in 4.2 mm rows. The read-back tests keep their 6/5 pt parameters, which both kinds of sheet meet.

## 6. Consistency and determinism

`drawing_set` computes the routed set from the same model and `DrawingSet`, so its rows, ends and labels are the schedules'. Every connection id is in exactly one of `routed`, `continued` (one flagged line on each of its two sheets) or `unrouted`. All orders are by schedule index or id and all limits are counts. The v2 renderer version is `2026.11.02.v2-10` (`2026.11.01.v2-7` before the readability round, `2026.11.02.v2-8` before the board qualifier, `2026.11.02.v2-9` before the final review fixes, Section 10); `drawing_routed_crowded` joins the v2 fingerprint cases.

M4 tests changed, with reasons: the geometry tests' lead, lead-reader and zone-reference checks apply to cross-reference sheets only (`Page.is_cross_reference`): a routed sheet has no symbol leads and its flags are checked by `test_routed_topology.py`. The open-item oracle accepts `unrouted` items, which are a routing outcome rather than a model fact, and checks them against `DrawingSet.routed`. The point column moved 5 mm right (`POINT` 41 mm, leads still end at 32 mm) to make room for the 8 mm stubs.

## 7. Test data

`drawing_routed_crowded` (added as data only: the JSON and its findings fingerprint entry): eight components and a 34-point strip on one board; crossing lines; a strip point joined three ways (junction); a group larger than one routed sheet, so lines continue across sheets; a common terminal with nine connections, two of them unrouted.

## 8. Decisions for the owner and a qualified engineer

M5-1 (lines acceptable, with the schematic wording), M5-3 (junction dots, hop crossings, dashed links: neutral, no standard claimed), M5-9 (one board per routed sheet run, lines continue across sheets by flags), M5-11 (budgets: seven per terminal, the channel's lanes) and M5-12 (unrouted is informational) are taken at the proposal's defaults and remain open.

## 9. Notes on decisions taken

- Continuation text for a strip point: the label is `<board>/<designation>:<marking>` without the side and level (the PDF contract's label has none); the side follows the parenthesis, `to XR:8 (sheet 4, zone 7E), inner side`.
- The `symbol reviewed (template approved)` rendering is untested by data: a submitted configuration may not claim a review, so no fixture has an approved template.

### 10a. Board qualifier (renderer `2026.11.02.v2-9`)

A cross-reference names a far end with its board (`<board>/<label>`) when the far end is on another board, as before, and now also whenever the end's sheet shows more than one board: a bare `K2:A2` on a sheet showing two boards with a `K2` could be either. A one-board sheet keeps the bare label for an end on its own board. The sheets a board is on are the ones the layout found in its previous round (the layout repeats until places settle; before any place is known, every end is qualified where boards may share sheets and the model has more than one board). Routed sheets show one board each and already name every flag's board. The topology and consistency oracles require it (`dec9529`).

Renderer version: what the v2 drawing PDF prints changed again, so `pp-design/2` is bumped from `2026.11.02.v2-8` to `2026.11.02.v2-9`, for the same reason as `v2-8`: `v2-8` is pushed but unreleased (on no deployed main), and re-recording it would let one version name two different drawings. v1 is untouched. Only v2 approvals recorded under `2026.11.02.v2-8` stop counting; they can exist only where `DESIGN_SNAPSHOT_SCHEMA` was `pp-design/2` on this unreleased branch, never in a deployed environment.

### 10b. Final review fixes (renderer `2026.11.02.v2-10`)

From the independent review of `c636a57`: (1) lines reached a moved or junction dot by a 2-3 mm vertical from a row away; every line now leaves its dot horizontally on the dot's own height and turns to its row outside the dot, see Fan-out (checked by `test_every_line_arrives_on_its_dots_row`); (2) the text of an unrouted stub sat about 0.3 mm from the next row's label; it now sits above its stub like a label, and labels of adjacent rows that would come within 1.5 mm are set side by side (`test_line_labels_and_stub_texts_keep_apart`); (3) the A3 print note on the cover and every routed sheet. Still: at a terminal with several lines the lines run very close together for the first 1-3 mm outside the dot before they turn to their rows.
